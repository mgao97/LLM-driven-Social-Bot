# LLM-driven-Social-Bot

### [1] Shangbin Feng, Herun Wan, Ningnan Wang, Zhaoxuan Tan, Minnan Luo, Yulia Tsvetkov (2024, ACL)

**[What Does the Bot Say? Opportunities and Risks of Large Language Models in Social Media Bot Detection](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.196/)**

This work investigates both the opportunities and risks of LLMs in social bot detection. It proposes a mixture-of-heterogeneous-experts framework for LLM-based bot detection, while showing that LLM-guided manipulation of textual and structured user information can substantially reduce the effectiveness and reliability of existing detectors.

### [2] Boyu Qiao, Kun Li, Wei Zhou, Shilong Li, Qianqian Lu, Songlin Hu (2025, AAAI)

**[BotSim: LLM-Powered Malicious Social Botnet Simulation](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33575)**

This work introduces BotSim, an LLM-powered simulation framework for modeling malicious social botnets in a dynamic social environment. LLM-driven agents autonomously generate posts, comments, and social interactions, and the resulting BotSim-24 benchmark demonstrates that detectors effective on traditional bot datasets perform substantially worse on LLM-driven bots.

### [3] Lynnette Hui Xian Ng, Kathleen M. Carley (2025, arXiv)

**[Are LLM-Powered Social Media Bots Realistic?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00998)**

This work investigates whether LLM-powered social media bots can realistically reproduce the linguistic, behavioral, and network characteristics of real social media users. The authors generate bot personas, posts, and interactions and compare the resulting synthetic networks with empirical bot and human networks, revealing systematic differences that have implications for both bot generation and detection.

### [4] Fanqi Kong, Xiaoyuan Zhang, Xinyu Chen, Yaodong Yang, Song-Chun Zhu, Xue Feng (2025, EMNLP)

**[Enhancing LLM-Based Social Bot via an Adversarial Learning Framework](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1185/)**

This work introduces EvoBot, an evolving LLM-based social bot that learns to generate increasingly human-like behavior through an adversarial learning framework. EvoBot is initialized with supervised fine-tuning and iteratively optimized with direct preference optimization using feedback from a co-adapting bot detector, enabling the bot to adapt to user profiles, social context, and detection strategies.

### [5] Lynnette Hui Xian Ng, Kathleen M. Carley (2025, arXiv)

**[The Dual Personas of Social Media Bots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12498)**

This work studies the diverse personas exhibited by social media bots and distinguishes bots based on their content and behavioral characteristics. It develops a taxonomy of bot personas and highlights that social bots should not be treated as a homogeneous population, motivating more fine-grained modeling of bot goals, behaviors, and identities.

### [6] Lynnette Hui Xian Ng, Kathleen M. Carley (2025, Scientific Reports)

**[A Global Comparison of Social Media Bot and Human Characteristics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96372-1)**

This work systematically compares social media bots and humans across linguistic, behavioral, and network characteristics. The findings show that bots and humans exhibit distinct communication and interaction patterns, demonstrating that effective bot detection should consider not only content but also temporal behavior and social network structure.

### [7] Duan et al. (2025, IEEE Transactions on Computational Social Systems)

**[LLM-BotGuard: A Novel Framework for Detecting LLM-Driven Bots With Mixture of Experts and Graph Neural Networks](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/)**

This work proposes LLM-BotGuard, which combines LLM-based semantic representations with graph neural networks to detect LLM-driven social bots. The framework uses a mixture-of-experts architecture to integrate heterogeneous user information and social structural signals, highlighting the importance of jointly modeling semantic and network-level evidence.

### [8] Duan et al. (2026, Information Fusion)

**[BotDMM: Dual-Channel Multi-Modal Learning for LLM-Driven Bot Detection on Social Media](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103758)**

This work develops a dual-channel multimodal framework for detecting LLM-driven social bots by jointly modeling user content and behavioral/structural information. It incorporates self-supervised learning to improve representation quality and evaluates the approach on both traditional and LLM-generated bot datasets.

### [9] BotLGT Authors (2025, Neurocomputing)

**[BotLGT: Social Bot Detection Based on LLM and Graph Transformer](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925231225021253)**

This work combines LLM-derived semantic representations with graph structural information for social bot detection. It introduces motif-based structural encoding and a graph transformer to capture both the semantic characteristics of users and their higher-order relationships in social networks.

### [10] BotEvo Authors (2026, Discover Computing)

**[BotEvo: LLM-Driven Social Bot Detection via Behavioral Evolution Modeling and Cross-Modal Fusion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44443-026-00814-3)**

This work focuses on the dynamic nature of LLM-driven social bots by explicitly modeling behavioral evolution over time. It combines evolving behavioral patterns with multimodal information to move beyond static bot classification toward dynamic and adaptive social bot detection.

### [11] Authors (2026, arXiv)

**[Breaking and Defending LLM-Powered Social Media Bot Detection Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15893)**

This work investigates adversarial attacks against LLM-powered social bot detection systems and shows that carefully designed attacks can substantially degrade detector performance. It further explores multi-LLM defense strategies, framing LLM-driven bot detection as an evolving arms race between adaptive bots and increasingly robust detectors.
